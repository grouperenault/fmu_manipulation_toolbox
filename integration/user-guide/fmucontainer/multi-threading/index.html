    Multi-Threading¶

    The multi-threaded mode of a container (-mt) runs the steps of its embedded Co-Simulation FMUs in parallel, on several threads. It shortens a simulation when some embedded FMUs have a long step; it brings nothing when all of them are fast, and the container then simply does not use threads.

    In short¶

    Situation Effect of -mt
    At least two embedded FMUs with a long step (≥ the threshold, 50 µs by default) Faster: their steps run at the same time, on worker threads
    A single FMU with a long step Small gain: its step overlaps the steps of the fast FMUs only
    All the FMUs are fast No effect: the container detects it and stays in the mono-thread mode
    Nested containers Enable -mt on one level only: the one whose FMUs are long
    -sequential is also given -mt is ignored: the sequential mode has no parallel step

    The results of the simulation are the same with and without -mt.

    How it works¶

    At each internal step, a container in parallel mode (the default) sets the inputs of all its embedded FMUs, steps all of them, then reads all their outputs: every FMU starts its step from the outputs of the others at the previous step. The multi-threaded mode changes who steps the FMUs, not this order. The inputs and the outputs are always exchanged by the main thread, before and after the steps, so the results do not depend on the threads.

    1. Warm-up: during the first 20 internal steps, the container runs in the mono-thread parallel mode and measures the duration of the step (doStep) of each Co-Simulation FMU.
    2. Dispatch: an FMU whose mean step takes at least the threshold is heavy, the others are light. The heavy FMUs are given to worker threads, at most as many as the cores minus one (the main thread keeps a core), the longest first, each to the least loaded worker.
    3. Steps: the workers step the heavy FMUs while the main thread steps the light ones; the step of the container ends when all of them are done.
    flowchart TD
    A["-mt: first 20 internal steps<br/>mono-thread, doStep of each FMU timed"] --> B{"Heavy FMUs<br/>(mean step ≥ threshold)?"}
    B -- "none" --> C["Mono-thread parallel mode<br/>(no thread started)"]
    B -- "1 or more" --> D{"More than one core?"}
    D -- "no" --> C
    D -- "yes" --> E["min(heavy FMUs, cores - 1) worker threads<br/>heavy FMUs: longest first, to the least loaded worker"]
    E --> F["Each step: workers step the heavy FMUs,<br/>the main thread steps the light ones"]

    The decision is taken once, at the end of the warm-up, and logged, for example:

    Container uses the parallel mode with several threads, for the FMUs whose step takes 50 us or more (measured over the first 20 steps).
Container uses the parallel mode with several threads: 4 worker thread(s) for 4 FMU(s), 8 FMU(s) on the main thread.
FMU 'Slow-0.fmu' (step of 101.3 us) runs on the worker thread #0.
FMU 'Slow-1.fmu' (step of 100.8 us) runs on the worker thread #1.
...

    or, when no FMU is heavy:

    Container uses the parallel mode with one thread: no FMU step takes the threshold.

    When it helps: several FMUs with a long step¶

    Take a container with two heavy FMUs, engine.fmu (300 µs per step) and gearbox.fmu (200 µs), and two light ones, sensor.fmu and controller.fmu (10 µs each). In the mono-thread mode, the steps follow each other: about 520 µs per internal step. With -mt, the two heavy FMUs run on two workers while the main thread steps the light ones: the step of the container lasts as long as the longest of them, about 300 µs.

    gantt
    title One internal step (1 unit = 10 µs)
    dateFormat X
    axisFormat %s
    section Mono-thread
    engine.fmu      : 0, 30
    gearbox.fmu     : 30, 50
    sensor.fmu      : 50, 51
    controller.fmu  : 51, 52
    section mt main thread
    sensor.fmu      : 0, 1
    controller.fmu  : 1, 2
    section mt worker 0
    engine.fmu      : 0, 30
    section mt worker 1
    gearbox.fmu     : 0, 20

    The step of the container is bounded by its longest FMU: two heavy FMUs of the same duration give the best gain, a heavy FMU much longer than the others leaves the workers of the others idle. With more heavy FMUs than workers, a worker steps several of them one after the other.

    Measured on a laptop (Apple M4, 10 cores) with 8 light FMUs and 4 heavy ones of about 100 µs each, 2 500 internal steps:

    Mode Time of the simulation
    Mono-thread (parallel) 1.05 s
    -mt 0.36 s (2.9 times faster)

    With a single heavy FMU, its step only overlaps the steps of the light FMUs: the gain is at most their duration, small when they are fast.

    When it does not help: all the FMUs are fast¶

    Releasing the worker threads and waiting for them costs a few microseconds at each step. When all the FMUs step in a few microseconds, running each of them on its own thread costs more than it saves: the simulation would be slower than in the mono-thread mode. The container avoids it: after the warm-up, no FMU is heavy, no thread is started, and the simulation goes on as without -mt.

    gantt
    title One internal step of 20 fast FMUs (schematic, 1 unit = 1 µs)
    dateFormat X
    axisFormat %s
    section Mono-thread
    20 FMUs one after the other   : 0, 19
    section One thread per FMU (not used)
    release the threads           : 0, 8
    steps in parallel             : 8, 12
    wait for all the threads      : 12, 30

    Measured with 20 fast FMUs (a few µs each), for the same simulation: 0.31 s in the mono-thread mode, 1.17 s with one thread per FMU (the behaviour of -mt before 2.1), 0.31 s with the current -mt. Enabling -mt costs nothing, but brings nothing either.

    Choosing the threshold¶

    The default threshold, 50 µs, is far above the cost of the synchronization of the threads (a few µs): an FMU above it is worth a thread. It can be changed:

    Where How
    Command line fmucontainer -container assembly.json -mt -mt-threshold 0.0002 (seconds)
    JSON description "mt": true, "mt_threshold": 0.0002
    Container Builder mt and mt_threshold in the parameters of the container
    AI Assistant set_container_options with mt and mt_threshold
    Python API container.make_fmu("assembly.fmu", mt=True, mt_threshold=0.0002), or AssemblyNode(..., mt=True, mt_threshold=0.0002)
    • 0 gives a thread to every Co-Simulation FMU (within the number of cores).
    • The log lists the heavy FMUs with the duration of their step: a good starting point to adjust the threshold. The profiling (-profile) gives the real-time ratio of each FMU over the whole simulation.
    • The steps are measured during the first 20 internal steps only: an FMU whose step is long at the start only (an initialization) or late only may be classified on this short sample.

    Nested containers¶

    A sub-container is, for its parent, one embedded FMU: its step contains the steps of all its own FMUs. With -mt, the parent measures it as any FMU, and gives it a worker if it is heavy.

    Each container that has mt keeps its own pool of workers, sized for the whole machine (cores - 1). Enabling mt on a container and on one of its sub-containers runs both pools at the same time, with more threads than cores: the threads wait for each other and the gain is lost. Enable mt on one level only, the one whose FMUs have long steps:

    flowchart TD
    subgraph root["vehicle.fmu: mt"]
        direction LR
        E["engine.fmu<br/>(sub-container, 400 µs)"]
        C["chassis.fmu<br/>(sub-container, 350 µs)"]
        D["driver.fmu<br/>(5 µs)"]
    end
    subgraph engine["engine.fmu: no mt"]
        direction LR
        E1["combustion.fmu"]
        E2["cooling.fmu"]
    end
    subgraph chassis["chassis.fmu: no mt"]
        direction LR
        C1["suspension.fmu"]
        C2["tires.fmu"]
    end
    E --- engine
    C --- chassis

    Here, the two sub-containers are the heavy FMUs of vehicle.fmu: they run on two workers, and each of them steps its own FMUs on its thread.

    {
  "name": "vehicle.fmu",
  "mt": true,
  "fmu": ["driver.fmu"],
  "container": [
    {"name": "engine.fmu", "fmu": ["combustion.fmu", "cooling.fmu"]},
    {"name": "chassis.fmu", "fmu": ["suspension.fmu", "tires.fmu"]}
  ]
}

    When the long FMUs are all in one sub-container (and its siblings are fast), enable mt on that sub-container instead. The AI Assistant warns about mt enabled on a container and on one of its ancestors (nested-mt).

    Limitations¶

    • Only the Co-Simulation FMUs are stepped by the workers: the Model-Exchange FMUs are integrated together by the main thread, after the steps of the Co-Simulation FMUs.
    • The inputs and the outputs of the FMUs are exchanged by the main thread: an FMU whose exchanges are slow (many ports, a remote FMU) does not get them in parallel.
    • -mt needs at least two embedded FMUs; with -sequential, it is ignored.
    • The dispatch is decided once, at the end of the warm-up, and kept until the end of the simulation, also after a reset of the container (fmi2Reset / fmi3Reset): there is no new warm-up.
